## Selected work

### CGM dashboard (GoodFlip)
As the sole designer, Daksh turned a real-time, medically dense glucose stream into something a non-technical, often elderly patient could trust, without dumbing it down for the coaches and doctors reading the same screens. His core bet was one dashboard serving several literacy levels through a per-metric call to show, simplify, or withhold, tiered across daily, cumulative, and shareable views. He wore the device himself for fifteen days to ground those decisions, and cut an AI camera install-assistant he had built once it proved too risky for medical accuracy, a judgment call he stands by. The work reached 77.6% end-to-end setup completion and 78% Day-14 retention. The full case study is on dakshxp.com.

### Kaira, food logging (GoodFlip)
Manual food logging was a weak spot in the product, so Daksh pitched replacing it with a conversational AI assistant, Kaira, and the team backed the bet. The real work was less about the chatbot and more about behaviour: studying how people actually talk about food, inferring rather than interrogating, giving Kaira a personality people wanted to come back to, and shipping in a way he could measure. It grew to around three logs per active user a day at roughly 90% positive feedback. The full case study is on dakshxp.com.

## Skills and how I work
Daksh is a B2C product designer at his core. On the UX side, he is patient with research and designs around emotion: he wants to know what a user is feeling at every step, not just where they tap, and he builds deliberate feedback loops into products to stay close to that (as seen in GoodFlip). Alongside that, he brings a strong sense of visual storytelling, treating UI as a way to carry a point of view rather than settling for point-and-click utility, which shows across his GoodFlip work, the Ring device experience, the CGM flow, and Kaira. He is also strong at working across teams: especially in smaller setups, he stays in direct contact with sales, operations, marketing, product, and engineering, reads what each team does best, and pulls the right people in to build the best outcome for the user. He takes initiative and learns fast, having repeatedly pitched new products, technologies, and directions during his time at TatvaCare, several of which shaped real decisions (covered in the CGM case study), and he keeps an eye on the numbers to see where things are heading. His toolkit spans design systems and automation, prototyping, product logic, and hands-on building in React and React Native, working in Figma and Miro, but the through-line is always an experience that feels alive and understands the person using it.

## How I stay close to users
Daksh uses several methods to stay close to his users. When it is possible, he talks to users directly for feedback, though in many B2C cases that is not feasible. When direct contact is not available, he gathers a group of people across different age groups and user types, often within the office, and interviews them as stand-ins for real users. He also leans on AI to collect feedback at scale, including AI-powered phone calls using tools like Bolna AI, which he used to gather data for the Kaira food-logging project. He treats people in sales and operations, anyone with a direct line to users, as a valuable source of insight, along with reviews on the Play Store, App Store, and other platforms. And he believes a UX designer should deliberately build feedback collection points into the product itself, something clearly visible in the GoodFlip app.

## Areas of growth
Two things Daksh is actively working on. First, having spent most of his career in B2C, he has had to stay close to users through proxies and feedback loops, insight from sales and ops, in-product signals, and AI-assisted user calls, rather than regular face-to-face conversations, and he is keen to join a team with more direct user access so he can test his read of users against the real thing more often. Second, he is a demanding critic of his own work, which keeps the bar high but can make him hold onto something longer than he needs to. He has been deliberately practicing shipping at a strong-enough point and iterating from there rather than waiting for perfect.
